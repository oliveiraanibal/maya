Maya Central Display Wireframes · iteration 1

Greyscale wireframes for the main screen: a large landscape central display, 16:10 (drawn as 1280 x 800). Screen IDs follow the order of the story. No colour or type decisions yet; this page decides layout, interaction patterns and components.

Iteration 1 · 27 October 2026. These greyscale wireframes came first and are kept as a record. The hi-fi screens replaced them, and these things changed:

  • Size: 1920 × 1200 instead of 1280 × 800.
  • Names: the screens are now called the central display and Panoramic Vision.
  • Screen IDs: renumbered in story order, S01 to S20 and C1 to C5. The old S1 to S9 below are the earlier names of the same screens.
  • No mic and no top status bar: Maya's bar at the bottom shows her status and opens the keyboard when tapped. The bento icon and "Maya" sit top left; the time, Errands and Settings sit top right.
  • Sign-in (S01) now comes before the welcome screen.
  • The briefing card is called "New plan", and its last slot is "To do".
  • The map is a real-looking map of Porto, not a diagram.
  • Courier mode (S18 to S20, C1 to C5) is new: the empty car collects the suit after the drop-off.

Kept from here: the layout strategy, the interaction patterns and the component kit.

1. Layout strategy

  1. Map first. A flat 2D map is always the stage. Panels and drawers sit beside or over it, so the passenger never loses "where are we and are we on time". Stops already passed turn grey on the map.
  2. One plan panel, always on the left. It holds the trip plan, ledger and alerts. Only one thing owns it at a time.
  3. Maya dock along the bottom, on every screen. Microphone, live caption and keyboard. Voice and touch are both one tap away, and anything Maya says is also written here.
  4. The keyboard opens from below and takes roughly the lower half of the screen; the plan panel and map shrink above it.
  5. Spare time pill pinned to the plan panel. It is the promise, so it never scrolls away.
  6. No modal dialogs. Changes appear as banners inside the panel. The only overlay is the incoming phone call, and it stays small.
  7. Large targets, short text. Controls are sized for arm's length on a console: aim for about 64 to 76 px high at 1280 x 800 (check against Android Automotive guidance at hi-fi). Two lines of text at most per card.
  8. Nothing duplicates Panoramic Vision. Speed, temperature and range stay on the Panoramic Vision; the central display shows no automotive data.
  9. Arrival is a different screen. The trip ends in a full-width report, not in the working layout.

4. Design system

Recommendation: decide at the hi-fi step, not now. The wireframes use a small kit of plain components, so the layout stays independent of any library. At hi-fi, use a free public system as a reference for sizes and behaviour, and build the brand layer (Maya, Panoramic Vision) on your own tokens. Candidates are Material 3 with the Android Automotive OS guidelines (free and public, includes in-car touch target and layout guidance). Adopting one whole would hide the decisions this exercise is meant to show.

5. Screens

6. Secondary screen: Panoramic Vision

A passive, non-touchable Panoramic Vision at the base of the windscreen, ultrawide (drawn at 14:1). It carries nothing unique: everything here is also on the central display. The left half is automotive data, the right half is Maya's. Short text only, about 20 characters, and colour is never the only signal.

  1. Left half: speed, temperature and range. Maya never writes here.
  2. Presence: a small dot that pulses. Filled while listening or on a call, outlined when idle.
  3. Next stop: icon, short label and arrival time. Updates as stops complete.
  4. Spare time: green above 4 min (solid), amber 0 to 4 (striped), red below 0 (double outline), always with the number.
  5. Progress beads: done (grey), current (filled), upcoming (outlined). Optional.

Events (4 seconds) take over slots 2 and 3 and then return. Never on Panoramic Vision: transcripts, maps, editing, anything long, medication names, personal data.

7. Decisions made

  1. The plan panel opens on the left in every market.
  2. The map is 2D.
  3. The keyboard opens from below and takes roughly the lower half of the screen.
  4. Only the latest ledger row has Undo; pressing it reveals Undo on the row before it. To validate in the prototype.
  5. Maya does not suggest answers before the user has replied.
  6. Pharmacy first: the first call is to a pharmacy on the route; the flowers offer comes after.
